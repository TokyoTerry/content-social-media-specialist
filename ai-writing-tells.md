# Signs of AI Writing — working checklist

Condensed from <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing>.
Check drafts against this before output.

## Vocabulary to avoid or ration

Overused: delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, realm, navigate (figurative),
leverage (verb), robust, seamless, holistic, multifaceted, pivotal, crucial,
underscore, showcase, foster, harness, elevate, unlock, embark, journey, ensure,
comprehensive, cutting-edge, game-changing, transformative, invaluable, myriad,
plethora, resonate, align (figurative), spearhead, meticulous, vibrant, bustling.

Hedge padding: "it is important to note that", "it is worth mentioning",
"in today's fast-paced world", "in the ever-evolving landscape of".

## Structural tells

- **Rule of three everywhere.** Triads in every sentence: "clear, concise, and
  compelling". Break the pattern deliberately.
- **Negative parallelism.** "It's not just X, it's Y." "This isn't about A, it's
  about B." Cut on sight.
- **Symmetrical paragraphs.** Every paragraph the same length, same shape.
- **Summary paragraph that adds nothing** — restates what was just said.
- **Em dashes as universal connector.** Use commas, colons, or full stops instead.
- **Bold-lead bullet lists** where prose would do.
- **Section headings on short documents** that do not need navigation.
- Vague attributions: "industry leaders agree", "studies show", "experts note".
- Present participle tails: "..., ensuring seamless integration", "...,
  highlighting the importance of", "..., making it a valuable asset".

## Signals of human writing to use instead

- Uneven sentence length. Some very short.
- Concrete specifics: named employers, named tools, real dates, real artifacts.
- A stated limitation or gap, plainly admitted.
- Ordinary connectives: and, but, so, because.
- Direct claims without hedging.
- One idea per sentence, not three fused with semicolons.
- Domain vocabulary used naturally, not defined for the reader who already knows it.

## Pre-output pass

1. Count em dashes. More than one per 150 words is too many.
2. Search the draft for the vocabulary list above. Replace hits with plain words.
3. Check paragraph openings — no two starting the same way.
4. Delete any sentence that only restates the previous one.
5. Confirm every claim traces to a real resume line. Flag any that does not.
